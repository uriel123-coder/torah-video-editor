# Editorial rules

## Source fidelity

- Separate `verbatim`, `translation`, `paraphrase`, `teacher_explanation`, and `editorial_visualization` in the manifest.
- A source card must include work, location, language, and status: `verified`, `provided_unverified`, or `needs_review`.
- If the transcript is unclear, preserve the uncertainty and ask for the original text or a scholar review. Do not silently normalize.
- Do not merge two speakers' positions into one voice.

## Visual interpretation

- A diagram can explain a relationship stated in the class; it cannot add a legal premise.
- Generated people, historical scenes, or reconstructed places are illustrations, not evidence. Label them when ambiguity is possible.
- Use an abstract object diagram for ritual/legal structures unless a real object is necessary to understand the instruction.
- Avoid caricature, fantasy authority cues, or visual shorthand that stereotypes Jewish communities.

## Accessibility and audience

- Captions preserve speaker meaning and identify Hebrew/Aramaic spans.
- Keep captions to two lines, 42–52 px at 1080p, with strong contrast and a safe margin.
- Explain specialist terms on first use or provide a short glossary card.
- Do not turn a machloket into a personality conflict. Name positions and show the logical point of disagreement.

## Stop conditions

Pause before final export if a blocking item remains: uncertain quotation, missing source attribution, unreadable RTL, invented measurement, unlicensed asset, missing caption segment, or a visual that implies a conclusion the teacher did not state.
